# LOUD — protest tracker (protesttracker.online)

Read `PLAN.md` §0 first (what changed from the original plan and why), then `DESIGN.md` for anything visual.

## Tone rules (verbatim from the plan — every session follows them)

The humour lives in the interface and the brand, never in how protesters, victims or causes are described. People get arrested, injured and killed at protests; one mocking card going viral would sink the brand.

1. Joke about the format, not the people. Playful labels, loading lines and empty states are fine ("No protests here. Suspiciously quiet."). Summaries of causes and demands stay neutral and factual.
2. Serious Mode switches on automatically. When an event reports deaths, serious injuries or mass arrests, its card drops the bright palette, stickers and jokes, and shows a calm grey-and-white layout. The AI pipeline sets a severity field that drives this.
3. Both sides, with receipts. Every summary states the protesters' demands and the government or authority response, each linked to sources. No adjectives like "brave" or "violent mob".
4. City-level locations only. Pins sit at city or district centroids, never exact gathering points or live movement. No protester names or faces.
5. India's official borders. Use a map provider with an India worldview so J&K and Arunachal display correctly.
6. Original text only. AI writes fresh summaries and links out; never copy article text or photos.
7. Confidence on every card. Show source count, a confidence score and "last verified" time. Unverified single-source reports stay hidden.

Owner decision (6 Oct 2026) on rule 7: reader reports from `/add` are the one exception to "single-source stays hidden". They show only after the automatic check (`pipeline/reader.ts`), always labelled "Reader report · not verified yet" (orange dashed card), noindex, no confidence score, no share card, never with claims of harm (those are held for newsrooms), and expire if newsrooms never confirm them.

Owner decision (11 Oct 2026) on rule 2: Serious Mode keeps its grey surface but gets a red border and a red SERIOUS label (`--serious-alert`); no other colour, stickers, tilt, motion or jokes come back.

Owner decision (24 Oct 2026) on review: with no Telegram reviewer configured, an event flagged only `low-confidence` (2 publishers = 70–75%) or `top-10` publishes at 2 independent publishers with its score shown (`score.ts`), at most 10 per country per 7 days of reporting, most-reported first (`RULES.twoSourcePerCountry`; the 42 over the cap on 24 Oct were withdrawn with `scripts/withdraw-events.ts`, which redirects to the country page); harm, disagreeing sources and tone words still need 3 publishers or a human.

Owner decision (31 Oct 2026) on scope: news that is not a protest but readers shouldn't miss (e.g. the Mbappé–China La Liga row, the Alaska Senate spoiler probe) goes up as a **Big Story** (`data/stories/<slug>.json`, `src/lib/stories.ts`, `/story/<slug>`, violet "NOT A PROTEST" band on the home page). Hand-picked and hand-written, never by the pipeline, under the same rules: neutral text, every side with its sources, ≥ 2 independent newsrooms (else not shown), the protest confidence formula, `verifiedAt` = when a human last checked every line. English only for now; off the home page 10 days after the last check, the page stays.

Owner decision (5 Nov 2026) on rule 2: Serious Mode also switches on at `RULES.massInjuries` (20) or more reported injured, police included, because the AI's serious-injuries flag missed 100 injured in Frankfurt. The same day the owner asked to track Europe closely (`pipeline/sweep.ts`) and to add China, as readers are there. China includes Hong Kong and Macau; Taiwan is left out, like Kosovo (rule 5).

Owner decision (5 Nov 2026) on ads: a slim closable bar at the bottom advertises the owner's KryVault (`src/components/Promo.astro`), shown under KryVault's own logo with no visible "Ad" word (the owner's choice; screen readers still hear "Advertisement" and the link stays `rel="sponsored"`), off Serious pages and `/add`, and never aimed at protesters or promising them safety. Clicks and closes are totals per day and country in D1 (`worker/promo.ts`, no IP or id): `npx wrangler d1 execute loud-reader-inbox --remote --command "SELECT * FROM promo_counts ORDER BY day DESC"`. Workers Builds doesn't apply migrations: run `npx wrangler d1 migrations apply loud-reader-inbox --remote` before pushing a new one.

House interpretation of rule 4: organisations, parties, elected officials and police/government spokespeople may be named; ordinary participants, students, the injured, detained or dead are never named.

## Architecture
- **Pipeline** (`pipeline/`, plain Node 22+ TypeScript, no build step): `run.ts` = discover (GDELT + RSS, a Google News search for other newsrooms on fresh single-source stories, `corroborate.ts`, then a search country by country in the local language for Europe and China, `sweep.ts`; leads only, they pass every check below) → fetch article → keyword gate → AI classify → geocode (GeoNames gazetteer) → match/create event → duplicate check (`dedupe.ts`: one AI call over same-city pairs with overlapping dates; same story → merged into the longest-live page, different → remembered in `distinctFrom`) → AI summary (≥ 2 independent publishers) → deterministic score (`score.ts`) → Telegram review → save.
- **Data** lives in `./data` on `main`, committed every 4 hours by the pipeline. `events/<id>.json` is the site's only input. Article text is never written to `data/`.
- **Site** (`src/`, Astro 7 static + Tailwind 4): reads `data/` at build. Domain/brand only in `src/site.ts`.
- **Countries** (`pipeline/config.ts` `COUNTRIES`, 66 since 5 Nov 2026: India, US, Canada, Australia, New Zealand, Japan, China (with Hong Kong and Macau), 44 in Europe incl. Ukraine/Russia/Turkey/Caucasus, 10 in the Middle East & North Africa, + PK/BD/NP/KE/ID). Kosovo is left out (India doesn't recognise it, rule 5); Palestine is `ps` with two GDELT codes (`fipsAlt`). Adding one = config entry + `npm run gazetteer` + a frame in `src/lib/geo.ts` `BBOX` + fr/de/en "in <country>" exceptions in `src/i18n/index.ts` + feeds in `pipeline/rss.ts` + a query in `pipeline/sweep.ts` if it is in Europe (and protest words in `util.ts` for a new language or script). The gazetteer has towns over 1,000 in Europe and the US, over 5,000 elsewhere. Country pages with no protests are `noindex` and left out of menus/sitemaps (`activeCountries`) until the pipeline publishes one. Hubs (`HUBS` in `src/lib/data.ts`): `/region/europe`, `/region/middle-east` and `/topic/ukraine-war` (cause `ukraine-war`, protests only — never fighting; `looksLikeProtest` drops war "strikes" before any AI call).
- **Readers** (Vercel Analytics, Oct 2026): search-driven, mostly US/France/Germany/UK; India is small. Never break a live URL: before shipping, every URL in the live sitemaps must still build or redirect. Search snippets (meta descriptions) never use the "Suspiciously quiet" joke: a quiet country/city/hub page leads with its latest checked protest (`meta.latest`); the joke stays on the page. Germans search "Demo", Spaniards "manifestación", so those titles use them; country titles say "map" (the page has one).
- **Reader reports**: `/add` form → `api/report.ts` (Vercel function) → private Vercel Blob store `loud-reader-inbox` (`queue/<day>/<iphash>-<n>.json`, 3/IP/day) → pipeline reads `api/inbox.ts` with the workflow's GitHub OIDC token (no shared secret) → `pipeline/reader.ts` checks reports ≥ 60 min old, publishes as `origin: 'reader'`, `stage: 'candidate'`; newsroom confirmation upgrades them via `summarize()`.
- **Languages**: English at `/`, Hindi/Marathi/Spanish/French/German under `/hi` `/mr` `/es` `/fr` `/de`. Interface text: `src/i18n/<lang>.ts` (same keys as `en.ts`, test enforces) and `src/i18n/how.ts` for the method page. Event text: `pipeline/translate.ts` translates the checked English once per change into `e.i18n` (never from source articles); an event page exists in a language only once translated. Page bodies live in `src/views/`; `src/pages/**` are thin wrappers generated by `scripts/make-lang-pages.mjs`. Components read the language from the URL (`langOf(Astro.url)`). City pages are `/city/<cc>/<region>/<city>` (old `/<cc>/...` URLs redirect).
- **Schedule**: every 4 hours (owner's choice 6 Nov 2026, was 3: private repo, 2,000 free Actions minutes/month, about 9 min per run since the Europe/China search, so 3-hourly was on course to run out in late November; no npm install in the workflow, and watch the usage on GitHub, the token here can't read it). GitHub's cron is unreliable on this repo, so a Cloudflare Cron Trigger calls `worker/tick.ts` every hour at :50, which dispatches the workflow if no real run started in 225 min (a scheduled run that skipped itself doesn't count); scheduled runs skip if one started in the last 3 hours. The "checks paused" banner shows after 6 hours (`src/scripts/live.ts`). Each run stops starting AI calls after 12 min (`aiMinutesPerRun`) so it always saves inside the 20-min job timeout, and a Gemini 503 switches to the fallback model after one retry. `worker/tick.ts` needs the Worker secret `GITHUB_DISPATCH_TOKEN` (fine-grained, Actions read/write, this repo); `api/tick.ts` is its Vercel copy, kept for rollback.
- **Deploy** (on Cloudflare since 28 Oct 2026, see `CLOUDFLARE-PLAN.md`; Vercel is kept for rollback with `ignoreCommand: exit 0`): `.github/workflows/pipeline.yml` every 4 hours → pipeline → commit `data/` to `main` → Cloudflare Workers Builds runs `npm run build` (whose last step pings IndexNow with the changed pages that exist in `dist/`) and `wrangler deploy` on every push.

## Rules for changing things
- Vibe/status/confidence/review numbers live in `pipeline/config.ts` `RULES` and are explained on `/how-we-know` — change both together.
- Prompts (`pipeline/prompts.ts`) encode the tone rules; never loosen them to get more events published.
- Never publish a number without a source (`summarize.ts` drops it) and never show single-publisher events.
- A slug never changes once set (URLs are forever). Wrapped-up events stay as archive pages. Duplicates: the pipeline merges same-city ones itself (`dedupe.ts`, since 2 Nov 2026, after Frankfurt had three live pages for one night); by hand, `node scripts/merge-events.ts <keepId> <dropId>` (moves articles, hides the duplicate, adds 301s in vercel.json); then run the pipeline to re-summarise. Either way `scripts/postbuild.ts` writes a redirect for every `mergedInto` event with a slug (to the country page if the kept one isn't shown), so a merge never leaves a dead URL. A slugless one-source candidate may be merged into a story in another city (a nationwide story announced city by city). A story the feeds missed: `node scripts/add-leads.ts <url>...` hands the pipeline leads (every check still applies; only the 12-day article-age gate is skipped).
- Maps: `scripts/build-map.ts` erases India's area from every other country (Natural Earth IND view overlaps). Re-run the PoK/Aksai Chin/Tawang point checks after touching it.
- Mobile first (most readers are on phones): every tap target ≥ 44 px, no sideways scroll at 320–414 px in any language (German/French words are long). Check with `npm run preview` + `PORT=4321 npm run audit:mobile` before shipping UI changes.
- Motion: only the three animations in DESIGN.md §7, all off under `prefers-reduced-motion`.
- `/about` and `/privacy` (text in `src/i18n/pages.ts`, all six languages, rendered by `src/components/InfoPage.astro`; publisher and contact email in `src/site.ts`). Privacy lists exactly what is collected: change it, and `INFO_UPDATED`, together with `worker/report.ts`, `worker/promo.ts`, `src/scripts/live.ts`, the analytics setup or the AI provider.
